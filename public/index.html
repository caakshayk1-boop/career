05 — Diagnosis

Why a hundred applications returned nothing

Ranked by how much each one cost you. The first is the big one and it is not the CV.

A hundred applications through job portals, from Malaysia, for roles in another country, is not a hundred attempts. It is one attempt at the lowest-yield channel there is, repeated a hundred times. Fixing the document raises the conversion on a channel that barely converts. Change the channel first.

Does it read as faking?

No. It reads as over-optimised, which in 2026 is discarded just as fast and for a worse reason — the reader assumes a model wrote it and stops attributing any of it to you.

The tells were specific: every achievement a superlative with a round number; seven claims of a perfect record; "Board- and C-suite-level financial intelligence", a phrase no working controller writes; and the same four facts repeated across summary, achievements and experience so the document was roughly 40% redundant.

Nothing in it was untrue. The problem was that everything was pitched at maximum, so a reader had no way to tell which two facts actually mattered. The rewrite keeps every claim and states each one once.

07 — Positioning

One sentence, three stories, six numbers

If you can say these without preparing, you are ready for any first-round call.

The sentence

"I am a Chartered Accountant running finance for Landmark Group's Malaysia and Indonesia retail businesses — about USD 95 million across 60-plus stores. I own the budget and forecast, consolidate both entities under IFRS and MPERS, and I led the finance side of our Dynamics 365 implementation, which took 40% out of the month-end close."

Four facts, no adjectives. Say it in under twenty seconds. Everything else in the call is a follow-up to one of those four.

Story 1 — The ERP

S Two countries closing separately, consolidation done by hand in spreadsheets.

T Finance lead for D365 across both entities.

A Rebuilt the chart of accounts so both entities mapped to one group structure, ran the migration and UAT, trained the teams, went live.

R Close time down 40%; manual consolidation gone entirely.

Use for: change management, systems, project leadership, "tell me about something you drove end to end".

Story 2 — The consolidation

S Two legal entities, two reporting frameworks, one group answer needed monthly.

T Own group consolidation under IFRS and MPERS.

A Standardised the reporting calendar and intercompany process, built the translation and elimination logic, held one close discipline across both countries.

R No restatements and no audit adjustments since taking ownership.

Use for: technical depth, controls, audit relationships, attention to detail.

Story 3 — Store economics

S 60+ stores, not all of them earning their lease.

T Make the portfolio's economics visible enough to act on.

A Store-level contribution margin analysis, then the renegotiate / relocate / close conversation with Retail Heads and Operations.

R Decisions taken on numbers rather than on tenure; group EBITDA protected.

Use for: commercial judgement, influencing without authority, "a time you disagreed with the business".

Six numbers to know cold

You will be asked for at least two of these in every first round. Hesitating on your own numbers costs more than getting a technical answer slightly wrong.

The message that gets answered

Not "are there any openings". That asks them to do work for you. This asks one specific question and gives them a reason to reply:

"Hi [name] — I run FP&A for Landmark's Malaysia and Indonesia business, about USD 95M across 60 stores. I am looking at moving to Dubai in the next few months and you made the same move a while back. Could I ask you two questions about how finance hiring actually works there — particularly whether groups are open to someone relocating, or whether they hold out for candidates already in-market?"

It is short, it names a shared context, it asks something only they can answer, and it does not ask for a job — so replying costs them nothing and refusing would feel odd. That is the whole mechanism. "Any openings?" fails because it asks a stranger to do your search for you.

09 — Market intel

The rule change that just repriced you

Verified at source on 2 September 2026. This is the single most useful thing on this page and most candidates in your position do not know it yet.

On 1 June 2026 Malaysia roughly doubled the minimum salary for every Employment Pass category. It applies to new and renewal applications alike. You told me Malaysia does not pay expats well at your level — that was true under the old policy. The floor has moved, and it moved in your favour.

What it means for you — the upside

  • Any Malaysian employer hiring you onto a new EP must clear the floor by law. You cannot be lowballed below it.
  • A genuine Financial Controller title is Category I territory. That anchors the conversation at RM 20,000/month — not at whatever you earn today.
  • It is a legal requirement, not a negotiating position. You are quoting the rule, not making a demand.

And the risk — check this week

  • Renewals are covered too. Find your EP expiry date and category. If your current salary sits under the new floor for your category, your own renewal is the problem, not just your next job.
  • That is a conversation to have with your employer before the renewal window, not during it.
  • Fewer employers will sponsor at the new levels. The pool shrinks; what remains pays more. Both are true.

Treat every published salary figure as a range, not a number. The sources above disagree by roughly two to one on the same job title, because "Financial Controller" spans a four-person company and a listed group. Use them to set your floor and your ask — never quote one back to a recruiter as if it were fact. The number that matters is the band the recruiter is actually briefed on, and asking for it directly is normal and expected.

The comparison that should drive the decision

A Malaysian Category I package at the new statutory floor is RM 20,000/month, taxed. Malaysian personal income tax at that level runs into the mid-twenties percent at the margin, so take-home lands materially below the gross.

A Dubai package at your own AED 30,000 target is ≈ RM 33,000/month, untaxed — and before any housing or schooling allowance, which is standard in Gulf packages and is not standard in Malaysian ones.

On gross alone the Dubai number is about 65% higher. After tax and allowances the gap is wider than that. That is the arbitrage, and it is why Dubai should stay the primary target with Malaysia as the well-paid fallback rather than the other way round.

Malaysian tax stated qualitatively on purpose — the exact liability depends on residency status, reliefs and filing position. Model your own before you compare offers.

10 — Money

Negotiation, in the order it actually happens

Most of the money is lost in the first two minutes of the first call, before anyone has said the word "offer".

Rule one — never name the first number

"What are your current salary and expectations?" arrives on the first recruiter call. Whoever names a number first sets the ceiling. You do not have to.

"I am looking at the whole package rather than the base in isolation — and Malaysian and UAE packages are not comparable line for line, given tax and allowances. What band is the role briefed at? If it is in the right area I am happy to go into detail."

This is not evasive; it is the correct answer, and any competent recruiter will simply tell you the band. If they refuse twice, that itself is information about the role.

Anchors — know these before you pick up

  • Malaysia, Category I floor: RM 20,000/month. Statutory, not aspirational.
  • Dubai, your target: AED 30,000/month, tax-free.
  • Dubai, recruiter-survey band: AED 35–45,000 for controller roles at established companies.
  • Your walk-away: decide it now, in writing, before the first call. A number decided under pressure is always too low.

What to itemise in a Gulf offer

Base alone tells you very little. Ask for every line before you compare:

  • Housing allowance — often 20–30% of base, sometimes paid annually up front
  • Schooling — the one that matters most with a young child, and the one most often omitted
  • Annual flights home, and for how many people
  • Medical cover, and whether dependants are included
  • Visa and relocation costs, for the whole family
  • End-of-service gratuity and the bonus structure — target and how it has actually paid

The one about the seven years

It will be asked in every interview, and the honest answer is the strong one. Do not apologise for the tenure and do not criticise Landmark.

"Seven years in one group is why I can do this job — I have owned a close, a consolidation and an ERP migration in the same business, and seen the consequences of my own decisions play out over years rather than quarters. What I want next is a bigger balance sheet and full controllership, and that step is not available where I am in the timeframe I want it."

It reframes long tenure as depth, states the ambition plainly, and gives a reason to leave that is about the role rather than about grievance.

11 — The long game

The part nobody schedules

A job search is eight to twelve weeks of rejection with no feedback. The people who land well are not the most qualified; they are the ones still executing in week ten.

Protect the process, not the outcome

You control three things: contacts made, drills done, applications targeted. You control none of the responses. Score yourself weekly on the three you control — 15 contacts, 15 drills, 15 targeted applications. A week where you hit all three and heard nothing back is a good week. Judging yourself on replies is how people quit in week six.

The rejection ledger

Log every rejection with one line on the likely reason. After ten, patterns appear that no single rejection shows: always after the salary conversation, always at the same stage, always the same objection. That is diagnosis. Without the log it is just a bad month.

Do not let the search eat the job

Your current role is the entire product you are selling, and your reference. A visibly checked-out FP&A Manager is a worse candidate and a worse referee. Search before work and at weekends; keep the day clean.

Tell your wife the plan, not the mood

Relocation with a small child is a joint decision with real costs — schooling, notice periods, one income during the move. Share the structure and the timeline rather than the daily emotional weather of it. The plan is reassuring; the mood is not.
