# The XYZ Bullet Formula

The formula Google's recruiting team popularised:

> **Accomplished [X], as measured by [Y], by doing [Z].**

X is the outcome. Y is the evidence. Z is the action that caused it. Written naturally, the order is usually Z-first or X-first — the formula is a checklist, not a sentence template. A bullet passes if a reader can answer all three questions.

## Why it works

Most CV bullets describe duties. Duties tell a recruiter what the job was; they say nothing about whether the person was good at it. "Responsible for social media" is a job description. "Grew Instagram following from 4K to 31K in seven months by shifting from static posts to a three-reels-a-week cadence" is evidence.

## Worked examples

**Weak:** Managed a team of retail stores.
**XYZ:** Lifted same-store revenue 18% across 12 Tier-3 franchise stores in nine months by rebuilding the sell-through review cadence and retraining 40 floor staff on assisted selling.

**Weak:** Handled customer complaints.
**XYZ:** Cut escalation-to-resolution time from 6 days to 34 hours by introducing a triage matrix and a same-day callback rule for the top two complaint categories.

**Weak:** Worked on the data migration project.
**XYZ:** Migrated 2.3M customer records to the new CRM with zero data loss by writing a field-mapping validator that caught 400+ schema mismatches before cutover.

**Weak:** Improved website performance.
**XYZ:** Reduced page load from 4.1s to 1.3s by lazy-loading below-fold images and moving static assets to a CDN, raising mobile conversion 22%.

## Handling missing numbers

Real resumes rarely carry clean metrics. Do not solve this by inventing one. Write the bullet with an explicit placeholder:

> Lifted same-store revenue by `[NEED NUMBER: % or ₹ change]` across `[NEED NUMBER: store count]` franchise stores by rebuilding the sell-through review cadence.

Placeholders are visible, specific about what's needed, and safe. A user who sends the CV without filling them looks careless. A user who sends a CV with a fabricated 34% looks like a liar in the interview. Careless is recoverable.

When the profile has no number and no reasonable proxy, prefer a scope or scale marker that is verifiably true — team size, geography, number of SKUs, budget range — over a fake outcome.

## Rules for every bullet

- Start with a strong past-tense verb. Not "Responsible for", not "Helped with", not "Worked on".
- One accomplishment per bullet. Two crammed together halves the impact of both.
- One to two lines. Three lines gets skimmed past.
- Vary the verbs across the CV. Six bullets starting with "Managed" reads as a template.
- Match the JD's seniority verbs where honest. If the JD says "own", and the person genuinely owned it, use "owned" rather than "supported".
- The number goes as close to the front as the sentence allows. Recruiters scan left.
- Four to six bullets for the current role, two to four for older ones, one to two for anything over eight years old.

## Verb bank by function

**Leadership:** led, owned, directed, built, scaled, restructured, hired, mentored
**Revenue:** grew, generated, closed, expanded, converted, upsold, retained
**Efficiency:** cut, reduced, streamlined, automated, consolidated, eliminated
**Build:** designed, launched, developed, implemented, deployed, integrated
**Analysis:** diagnosed, forecast, modelled, audited, benchmarked, identified
